## Outreach templates

### Email
**Subject**: Industrial sewing machines for [Company] — quick question
**Opening**: "Hi [Name], I came across [Company] while researching
garment manufacturers in [city]."
**Why contacting them**: "[Specific real observation — e.g. 'saw you
manufacture shirts/wovens' or 'saw your export capacity listing']."
**Swami introduction**: "We're Swami Enterprises, an authorized JACK
dealer in Delhi since 2015 — we supply and support industrial sewing
machines for garment units across the city."
**Simple question**: "Are you currently evaluating any new machines, or
is your current setup working well?"
**CTA**: "Happy to share specs/pricing if useful — no pressure either
way."

### Phone
**Opening**: "Hi, this is [name] from Swami Enterprises — we're an
authorized JACK dealer in Delhi. Is now an OK time for a quick question?"
**Qualification**: "What are you currently producing, and roughly what
scale?"
**Problem discovery**: "Any issues with your current machines, or
planning to add capacity?"
**Machine discussion**: only after the above — never lead with a
product pitch before understanding their actual need.
**CTA**: "Would it help if I sent over some specs on WhatsApp?"

### LinkedIn
Short, no hard sell: "Hi [Name] — we work with garment units in Delhi on
industrial sewing machine selection (authorized JACK dealer since 2015).
Happy to connect if useful for [Company]'s production side."

## Qualification script (max 7 questions, real discovery not interrogation)

1. What are you manufacturing?
2. What type of production (export, domestic, both)?
3. What machines are you currently using?
4. What's the current problem, if any?
5. Are you adding or replacing machines?
6. Roughly how many machines would this be?
7. What's the timeline?

Never ask all 7 in one breath — this is a conversation guide, not a
checklist to read aloud.

## Follow-up sequence

| Day | Action | What it adds (never "just following up") |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Initial contact (call/email/LinkedIn) | First real value: a specific observation about their business |
| 2 | Follow-up | A relevant guide link (e.g. the knitwear or shirt-factory guide, whichever matches) |
| 5 | Follow-up | A specific machine recommendation, if enough was learned on day 0 |
| 10 | Follow-up | Comparison info (e.g. F6 vs 2002G) if they're deciding between options |
| 21 | Final follow-up | Direct availability/pricing conversation, or move to NURTURE status |
